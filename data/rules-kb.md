## POINT-VALUES — What each scoring item is worth
section: Scoring

Scoring in Override: each Scored alliance-colored Pin is 5 points; each Scored
yellow Pin that your alliance Owns is 10 points; each of your Robots in the
Midfield at the end of the Match is 8 points; and the Autonomous Bonus is 12
points (6 to each alliance on a tie).

## G1 — Treat everyone with respect
section: Conduct

Everyone at an event is expected to act with courtesy and good sportsmanship.
Disrespectful or unsporting behavior can lead to warnings, penalties, or
disqualification.

## G2 — Student-centered program
section: Conduct

V5RC is meant to be run by the students. Adults may teach and mentor, but should
not design, build, or code the competition robot for the team. Robot plans and
code provided by VEX Robotics may be used as a starting point.

## G3 — Use common sense
section: Conduct

When the manual doesn't explicitly cover a situation, apply common sense and the
spirit of the game rather than looking for loopholes.

## G4 — Work must represent the team's skill level
section: Conduct

The robot must reflect the skills of the students on the team — no outside or
adult work. If a team member leaves, the remaining team is still expected to
understand and maintain the robot.

## G5 — One team per student
section: Conduct

Each student may belong to only one competition team.

## G6 — Accidental vs. willful rule violations
section: Conduct

Any robot-rule violation (accidental or not) means the team can't play until it
passes inspection again. Deliberately circumventing or breaking rules to gain an
advantage also violates the spirit of the competition.

## S1 — Be safe
section: Safety

Safety comes first. Unsafe behavior or robots will be addressed by event staff.

## S2 — Students must be accompanied by an adult
section: Safety

Students must have an adult present with them at the event.

## S3 — Participant release form required
section: Safety

Each student team member must have a completed participant release form on file.

## S4 — Stay inside the field
section: Safety

Keep within the designated field and competition areas as directed; don't reach
into or enter the field during a match except where the rules allow.

## S5 — Wear safety glasses
section: Safety

Safety glasses must be worn in the required areas around the field and pits.

## GG1 — Only drive team members, only in the alliance station
section: General game

During a match, only the designated drive team members may take part, and they
must operate from their alliance station.

## GG2 — Attend every match
section: General game

A team's robot is expected to show up for every scheduled match; a match will be
played whether or not you appear.

## GG3 — Robots must be ready to play
section: General game

When placed on the field a robot must be match-ready: battery charged, within the
starting-size limit, and displaying the correct alliance-color license plates.

## GG4 — Hands out of the field
section: General game

During a match, drive team members may not intentionally touch field elements,
robots, or scoring objects, except for the limited contact the rules permit (such
as legally introducing match loads).

## GG5 — Match replays are rare
section: General game

Replaying a match from the start only happens in extreme circumstances (e.g. a
match-affecting field fault) and must be agreed to by both the event partner and
head referee.

## GG6 — Disqualifications
section: General game

A disqualified team scores zero for that match and earns zero win points,
autonomous win points, and autonomous points. The opposing (non-DQ'd) alliance
generally receives the win.

## GG7 — Time-outs
section: General game

Each elimination alliance gets one three-minute time-out to use between
elimination matches. There are no time-outs during qualification matches.

## GG8 — Keep your robots together
section: General game

A robot must remain a single unit — it may not intentionally separate into
multiple pieces or leave parts behind on the field.

## GG9 — Don't hook to the field or get entangled
section: General game

Robots may not intentionally grab, hook, latch onto, or become entangled with
field elements. This protects the field and prevents anchoring.

## GG10 — Red alliance may place last
section: General game

The red alliance may choose to position its robots after blue, giving red a
placement advantage.

## GG11 — Controllers must stay connected to the field
section: General game

Controllers must be connected to the field control system during the match.

## GG12 — Autonomous means "no humans"
section: General game

During the autonomous period, drivers may not control the robot — it runs only on
pre-programmed code with no human input.

## GG13 — All rules still apply in autonomous
section: General game

Every game and robot rule applies during the autonomous period, not just the
driver-controlled period.

## GG14 — Don't destroy other robots, but expect defense
section: General game

Intentionally damaging or destroying opponents is prohibited, but defense and
incidental robot-to-robot contact are a normal part of the game.

## GG15 — Offensive robots get the benefit of the doubt
section: General game

When a judgment call is close, referees tend to favor the robot that was playing
offense (scoring) over the one playing defense.

## GG16 — You can't force an opponent into a penalty
section: General game

You may not deliberately maneuver so that an opponent is forced to break a rule.

## GG17 — No holding for more than a 3-count
section: General game

During the driver-controlled period you may not hold/trap an opposing robot for
more than about three seconds (a referee "count"). Separate by roughly one tile
(~2 feet) to reset the count.

## GG18 — Use scoring objects to play the game
section: General game

Scoring objects are meant to be used to play and score, not hoarded or used to
improperly interfere with the game.

## SC1 — Scoring is evaluated after the match
section: Scoring

All scoring statuses are judged after the match ends, based on the final state of
the field.

## SC2 — What counts as a "Placed" scoring object
section: Scoring

A Pin or Cup counts as Placed when it is nested inside a Goal or stacked on
another already-placed piece — not just loose on the field.

## SC3 — Scored Pin halves
section: Scoring

Each Pin has two halves (red, blue, or yellow). A half counts as Scored for its
alliance only if it's on a Placed Pin and remains fully visible (not hidden
inside the opaque half of a Cup). Red halves score for red, blue for blue, and
yellow halves score for whichever alliance Owns the Pin.

## SC4 — When a Toggle is set to a color
section: Scoring

A quadrant's Toggle counts as set to an alliance's color only when two things are
true: it is fully seated (a face resting flat against and parallel to its mounts
on the field perimeter), and it isn't being touched by a robot from either
alliance. Otherwise the Toggle is treated as neutral (yellow) and neither
alliance owns that quadrant's yellow Pins.

## SC5 — Yellow Pin ownership
section: Scoring

A Placed yellow Pin is Owned by the alliance whose color that quadrant's Toggle is
set to. If the Toggle is set to yellow, the yellow Pins in that quadrant are not
owned and score nothing.

## SC6 — Midfield ownership
section: Scoring

Whichever alliance ends the match with more robots in the Midfield owns the
yellow Pins placed in the Midfield goal. If the robot count is tied, those yellow
Pins are not owned and score nothing.

## SC7 — Autonomous Bonus
section: Scoring

The alliance with the higher score at the end of the 15-second autonomous period
earns a 12-point Autonomous Bonus; if tied, each alliance gets 6 points.

## SC8 — Autonomous Win Point
section: Scoring

An alliance earns the Autonomous Win Point by ending the autonomous period, with
no violations, having: at least 7 Pins scored for the alliance; at least 3 Goals
each holding 2+ of the alliance's scored Pins; and neither robot touching the
field perimeter. Pins/Goals in quadrants on the opponents' side of the autonomous
line don't count. Criteria are slightly tougher at events that qualify directly
to the World Championship.

## SG1 — Starting a match
section: Specific game

Robots begin stationary in a legal starting position, each with its one preload,
sized within the starting-size limit. A robot holding excess scoring objects at
the start faces restrictions until it's legal.

## SG2 — Horizontal expansion limit
section: Specific game

After the match starts a robot may expand horizontally, but must always fit
within a 24" × 24" footprint at every point in the match — including while
vertical mechanisms are deploying.

## SG3 — Vertical expansion limit
section: Specific game

After the match starts a robot may expand upward, but no part may ever exceed 50"
tall — except for the Midfield endgame exception (see SG12).

## SG4 — Keep scoring objects in the field
section: Specific game

Don't launch, throw, or otherwise send scoring objects out of the field.

## SG5 — One Pin preload
section: Specific game

Each robot starts the match with exactly one Pin as its preload.

## SG6 — Possession limit
section: Specific game

A robot may possess at most one Pin and one Cup at a time. If it ends up with
more, it must stop everything except getting rid of the extra object (or return
to a legal starting position).

## SG7 — Don't cross the autonomous line or interfere during auton
section: Specific game

During autonomous, don't cross the autonomous line onto the opponents' side and
don't interfere with the opponents' actions or side of the field.

## SG8 — Midfield/autonomous line during auton is at your own risk
section: Specific game

If you engage with the Midfield or autonomous line during the autonomous period,
you do so at your own risk — the usual protections don't apply there.

## SG9 — Alliance Goals are protected
section: Specific game

Your alliance's own Goals are protected: opponents may not descore from or
interfere with the scoring objects in them.

## SG10 — Can't remove objects from neutral or opponent Goals
section: Specific game

You may not remove or descore scoring objects from a neutral Goal or an
opponent's alliance Goal.

## SG11 — Introducing Match Loads
section: Specific game

Additional scoring objects (Match Loads) may be added to the field during the
match only under the specific conditions the rules allow (through the Loaders).

## SG12 — Endgame rule changes
section: Specific game

In the final endgame, a robot inside the Midfield must keep its height close to
its approximate starting height. A robot that clearly over-expands there gets a
warning and must lower or leave, or risk a violation.

## SG13 — Load Zones protected during driver control
section: Specific game

During the driver-controlled period, Load Zones are protected areas that
opponents may not interfere with.

## R1 — One robot per team
section: Robot

Each team competes with a single robot.

## R2 — Robots must pass inspection
section: Robot

A robot must pass inspection before it can compete; it can't play until it does.

## R3 — 18" starting size
section: Robot

At the start of a match the robot must fit within an 18" × 18" × 18" cube. Any
restraints used to hold starting size must stay attached to the robot all match.

## R4 — Display team numbers on license plates
section: Robot

The team's officially registered number must be shown on the robot's license
plates in the correct alliance color.

## R5 — Let go of scoring objects after the match
section: Robot

Once the match ends, robots must release any scoring objects they're holding so
scoring can be evaluated.

## R6 — One Brain
section: Robot

A robot uses exactly one V5 Robot Brain.

## R7 — Keep power accessible
section: Robot

The power button or battery connection must remain accessible on the robot.

## R8 — Firmware
section: Robot

Robot devices must run up-to-date firmware.

## R9 — Use a competition template
section: Robot

Programs must be built on the official Competition Template so the robot responds
correctly to field control (autonomous/driver phases, enable/disable).

## R10 — Motor limit
section: Robot

Robots may use any mix of V5 Smart Motors (11W and 5.5W), but the combined power
of all motors on the robot may not exceed 88W — counting even motors that aren't
plugged in. Only Smart Motors on Smart Ports may drive the robot.

## R11 — Subsystem 2 motor limit
section: Robot

On top of the overall 88W robot motor limit, the V5 Smart Motors making up
Subsystem 2 may total at most 55W of combined power, and those motors must stay
dedicated to their own subsystem rather than being rigged to power the rest of
the robot.

## R12 — VEX batteries only
section: Robot

Electrical power may come only from legal VEX batteries.

## R13 — Robots use VEXnet
section: Robot

Robots communicate over VEXnet (V5 radio).

## R14 — Give the radio space
section: Robot

Mount the V5 radio with enough clearance for a reliable connection, per the
guidance.

## R15 — One or two controllers
section: Robot

A robot is operated with one or two VEX controllers.

## R16 — Built from the VEX V5 system
section: Robot

Robots are built from the official VEX V5 product line (with the limited
exceptions the R-rules allow).

## R17 — New VEX parts are legal
section: Robot

Newly released official VEX parts are legal for competition once available.

## R18 — Prohibited items
section: Robot

Certain items are banned (e.g. anything unsafe, damaging, or otherwise
disallowed). Prohibited components may not be on the robot.

## R19 — Some non-VEX components allowed
section: Robot

A limited set of specific non-VEX components is permitted within the rules.

## R20 — Custom V5 Smart Cables allowed
section: Robot

Teams may make their own V5 Smart Cables within the allowed specifications.

## R21 — Limited tape allowed
section: Robot

A limited amount of tape is permitted on the robot.

## R22 — Some non-VEX fasteners allowed
section: Robot

Certain non-VEX fasteners (e.g. specific screws/nuts) are allowed within limits.

## R23 — Visual decorations allowed
section: Robot

Non-functional visual decorations are permitted. Changing the color of legal VEX
parts (anodizing, painting, dyeing) is allowed, with a regional exception.

## R24 — Limited custom plastic
section: Robot

A limited amount of team-made custom plastic is allowed within the specified
thickness/area limits.

## R25 — Pneumatics limited
section: Robot

A robot's pneumatics may use at most two VEX air tanks, charged to no more than
100 psi, used only to actuate legal pneumatic devices. No other means of storing
or generating pressure is allowed.

## R26 — Pressure gauge required with pneumatics
section: Robot

If a robot uses pneumatics, it must include the VEX pressure gauge so pressure can
be checked.

## R27 — Most non-electrical modifications allowed
section: Robot

Most modifications to non-electrical components are permitted (cutting, drilling,
etc., within the rules).

## R28 — No modifying electronics or pneumatics
section: Robot

Electronic and pneumatic components may not be modified in any way.

## RSC1 — Standard rules apply in Skills
section: Robot Skills

Robot Skills matches use the standard game and robot rules except where the RSC
rules say otherwise.

## RSC2 — Skills match play differs
section: Robot Skills

Robot Skills are played by a single robot alone (no opponents), in Driving Skills
and Autonomous Coding Skills formats.

## RSC3 — Scoring Skills matches
section: Robot Skills

Skills matches are scored from a scoring table; a robot can earn points for Pins
of any color (there's no opponent alliance).

## RSC4 — Skills field setup
section: Robot Skills

Skills fields are set up per the manual's Skills configuration.

## RSC5 — Skills Stop Time
section: Robot Skills

A team may end an autonomous skills run early to record a Skills Stop Time, used
as a tiebreaker.

The head referee has ultimate, final authority over gameplay and robot rulings at
an event.

## T2 — Head referees must be qualified
section: Tournament

Head referees must be appropriately certified/qualified.

## T3 — Right to appeal a ruling

Drive team members may immediately (and respectfully) appeal a head referee's
ruling at the time it's made.

## T4 — Event partner authority on non-gameplay decisions

The event partner has final authority over non-gameplay matters at the event.

## T5 — Expect minor field variance
section: Tournament

Teams should be prepared for small, allowable variations between fields.

## T6 — Fields may be repaired
section: Tournament

Fields may be repaired at the event partner's discretion during an event.

## T7 — Fields must be consistent
section: Tournament

All fields at an event must be set up consistently with each other.

## T8 — Field control types
section: Tournament

There are three permitted types of field control that events may use.

## T9 — Field perimeter types
section: Tournament

There are two permitted types of field perimeter.

## T10 — Qualification matches follow the schedule
section: Tournament

Qualification matches are played according to the published match schedule.

## T11 — At least six qualification matches
section: Tournament

Each team is scheduled for at least six qualification matches.

## T12 — Qualification matches set ranking

Qualification results determine each team's ranking used for alliance selection.

## T13 — Ranking tiebreakers
section: Tournament

When teams are tied in ranking, the manual's defined tiebreakers decide the order.

## T14 — Small tournaments have fewer alliances

Smaller tournaments run their elimination bracket with fewer alliances.

## T15 — Send a student rep to alliance selection
section: Tournament

A student representative must attend alliance selection to accept/decline/invite.

## T16 — Invited once to one alliance
section: Tournament

During alliance selection, each team may be invited only once and may join only
one alliance.

## T17 — Elimination matches follow the bracket
section: Tournament

Elimination matches proceed according to the elimination bracket seeding.

## T18 — Elimination format
section: Tournament

Elimination matches use a blend of best-of-1 and best-of-3 formats as defined by
the bracket stage.

## T19 — Ties in elimination
section: Tournament

Ties in elimination matches lead to limited rematches to break the tie.

## T20 — Skills match schedule
section: Tournament

Robot Skills matches are run on their own schedule at an event.

## T21 — Skills field modifications
section: Tournament

Skills challenge fields don't require the same modifications as head-to-head
match fields.

## T22 — Skills rankings at events
section: Tournament

Skills rankings at an event combine a team's best driving and autonomous runs.

## T23 — Global skills rankings

Skills scores also feed into global season-long rankings.

Robot Skills at league events follow the manual's league-specific handling.
